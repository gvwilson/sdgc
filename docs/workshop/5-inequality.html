Inequality

Greg Wilson

2026

Opening question

  • When you explain why someone succeeded or failed, which verbs do you reach for?
  • Our default is to explain others by their traits and ourselves by circumstance
  • This asymmetry is the doorway into structural explanation

Three dimensions of stratification [Weber1946]

  • Class: economic position
  • Status: prestige and honor
  • Power: capacity to realize one’s will
  • They overlap but do not coincide

Mismatches matter

  • A staff engineer may have high class but limited power
  • A founder has power
  • A moderator has neither
  • High income and low security can coexist in the same role

More unequal societies [Wilkinson2011]

  • More unequal societies do worse on nearly every social indicator
  • The mechanism is status anxiety
  • People compare themselves upward
  • The gap itself is the harm, not just the average level

Structural vs. individual

  • The fundamental attribution error applied to society
  • We explain others’ outcomes by their traits and our own by circumstance
  • Individual explanation is the tech default
  • Structural explanation asks what the rules, networks, and positions did

Cumulative advantage and opportunity hoarding

  • Cumulative advantage: early advantage compounds over a lifetime
  • The Matthew effect: advantage begets advantage
  • Opportunity hoarding: “merit” filters are built to reproduce advantage [Tilly1998]
  • Credentials, networks, and gatekeeping do the sorting while appearing to reward merit

Intersectionality [Crenshaw1989]

  • Overlapping disadvantage interacts; it does not simply add
  • A facial-recognition model or hiring algorithm distributes harm unevenly across identities
  • Single-axis analysis misses the worst harms
  • Race, class, gender, and disability compound rather than stack

Discrimination made concrete: audit studies

  • Identical resumes with white-coded names get more callbacks
  • Rivera’s “polished” versus “rough around the edges” is class, not competence
  • The name at the top changes the outcome
  • The process looks neutral while the result is not

Discrimination made concrete: tech

  • Amazon’s recruiting tool penalized resumes containing “women’s” (abandoned 2018)
  • Women’s share of computing was higher in the 1980s than today
  • The “pipeline” explanation is contradicted by the evidence
  • Becker’s 1957 prediction that competition eliminates discrimination was falsified

The pencil test

  • South Africa’s Race Classification Board used a pencil in the hair to sort people
  • Siblings could be classified differently, with different rights
  • The “Hispanic” category was invented by the Nixon administration in 1970
  • Categories are made, not found

Poverty is a tax on cognition [Mullainathan2013]

  • Scarcity depletes cognitive bandwidth the way sleep deprivation does
  • McIntosh’s invisible knapsack explains why “just try harder” fails [McIntosh1989]
  • Credit bureaus collect without consent and profit whether the data is accurate or not
  • Equifax 2017 exposed roughly 147 million people; accuracy is not fairness

Meritocracy, examined [Young1958]

  • The word was coined in a 1958 satire by Michael Young
  • Merit-pay organizations show larger gender pay gaps via moral licensing [Castilla2010]
  • Believing in meritocracy predicts harsher judgment of the poor
  • The term legitimizes the inheritance it claims to have replaced

Merit badges and hoarding

  • Carnegie: ten workers killed by Pinkertons in 1892, then a lifetime funding libraries on his terms
  • The Flexner Report raised medical standards and closed most schools that trained Black physicians or admitted women
  • Both are opportunity hoarding wearing a merit badge
  • Waring: a woman cooking for her family adds nothing to GDP, but hiring a cook raises it twice [Waring1988]

Takeaway

  • Inequality is not a sorting of individuals by merit
  • It is the accumulated effect of rules, networks, and compounding advantage
  • Mobility is low and sticky across generations
  • Meritocracy is a claim to test, and often a story that legitimizes inheritance
