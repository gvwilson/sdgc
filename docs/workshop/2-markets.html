Where Markets Come From

Greg Wilson

2026

Opening question

  • Which product in this room is unregulated?
  • The answer is none
  • Every product rests on rules about property, contract, fraud, and liability
  • “Deregulation” is really re-regulation

Markets are constructed [Polanyi2001]

  • Markets are always embedded in a web of legal, social, and political rules
  • Property rights, enforceable contracts, currency, and liability all rest on the state
  • Deregulation is not the removal of rules; it is the substitution of one set of rules for another
  • The cocaine cartel is a market with property rights enforced by violence instead of trademark law

The barter myth [Graeber2011]

  • Credit and debt predate money
  • Money emerged to settle debts, not to simplify barter
  • In 1660s London, goldsmith receipts circulated as money
  • When a loan is repaid, the deposit disappears and money is destroyed

Limited liability and copyright

  • Limited liability is a grant from the state (Dutch East India Company, 1602)
  • The Statute of Anne (1710) created copyright to break the Stationers’ monopoly, not to protect authors
  • The 1998 term extension was timed to keep Mickey Mouse out of the public domain
  • These rules are choices with distributional consequences

Homo economicus

  • “Homo economicus” is a political argument dressed as math
  • From Hobbes to Spencer’s “survival of the fittest” to the rational agent
  • It describes one kind of market as if it were the only kind
  • The firm we treat as natural is one choice among many

Four market failures

  • Externalities: costs imposed on third parties that the price does not capture
  • Public goods: non-excludable and non-rivalrous, so no one can be made to pay
  • Information asymmetry: one party knows much more than the other [Akerlof1978]
  • Market power: network effects and switching costs produce concentration

Externalities and public goods in tech

  • Externalities: misinformation, polarization, and teen anxiety are costs priced at zero
  • Public goods: an informed public and institutional trust
  • No single company has an incentive to produce trust
  • That is an argument about who should produce it

Information asymmetry and market power

  • You do not know what the recommender is doing, or why
  • The “market for lemons”: bad options drive out good ones [Akerlof1978]
  • Network effects make dominant social networks hard to exit
  • Market power is the ability to set terms rather than take them

The commons as a fifth idea [Ostrom2015]

  • Hardin described an unmanaged commons he never studied
  • Ostrom’s design principles show communities can govern shared resources without markets or the state
  • Open-source projects and Wikipedia are governed commons
  • The tragedy is not inevitable; it is a design choice

Apple’s green bubbles

  • Internal documents admit RCS would reduce the social cost of switching
  • The green-bubble lock-in is market power, not a protocol detail
  • The DMA forced support in 2024
  • A design choice can be a barrier to entry

Public subsidy, private profit

  • Every technology in the iPhone was government-funded: DARPA internet, GPS, Siri
  • The mRNA vaccine produced Moderna’s roughly $200 billion while the NIH’s IP claim went unenforced
  • Apple paid 0.005 percent tax on Irish profits
  • The public paid the research bill; private owners captured the profit

Efficiency is not fairness

  • A market can be efficient and still concentrate benefits and diffuse harms
  • Henry Ford paid his workers enough to buy his cars
  • Block’s 2025 layoffs were rewarded with a 25 percent stock bump
  • Automation beyond the socially optimal level is rational for the firm, costly for everyone else

Enshittification [Doctorow2025]

  • Platforms first attract users, then extract value from users and their suppliers
  • The audience is the crop; the algorithm change is the landlord raising the rent after the harvest
  • Booking.com’s commission rose from 12 to 25-30 percent
  • Amazon Basics competes with its own sellers using their sales data

The enshittification ladder

  • Columbia House’s penny records
  • YouTube’s 2018 monetization threshold
  • JanSport’s backpacks
  • Each rung is a small extraction that no single user will quit over

Institutional variety

  • Beyond the shareholder firm: the Islamic waqf, keiretsu, the Mittelstand, the Hindu Undivided Family
  • The waqf covered roughly a third of Ottoman farmland
  • The Hindu Undivided Family structure ran Tata and Birla
  • Cooperatives and the Mittelstand are durable institutions, not seminar hypotheticals

The MLM warning

  • The FTC found under 1 percent of MLM participants earned a net profit
  • Uber pitched drivers as “entrepreneurs” while setting the terms
  • The structure that looks like opportunity is often extraction
  • Fake competition is worse than monopoly

Kayfabe

  • Apple and Google look like rivals
  • Apple earns roughly $20 billion a year from making Google the default search engine on the iPhone
  • The two rivals are financially interdependent
  • Each needs the other to play its designated role

Bubbles and the greater fool

  • A single Semper Augustus tulip bulb sold for the price of a canal house
  • Newton lost a fortune in the South Sea Bubble: “I can calculate the motions of heavenly bodies, but not the madness of men”
  • A bubble collapses when no greater fool can be found
  • Markets move on contagious stories, not just fundamentals

Takeaway

  • There is no market without rules
  • The interesting questions are which rules exist, who wrote them, and who benefits
  • When markets fail, regulation is the standard correction, not an alien intrusion
