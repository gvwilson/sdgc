Belief

Greg Wilson

2026

Opening question

  • What did you learn about the world this week, and who decided it was worth your attention?
  • Belief is produced, not just discovered
  • Whoever controls the agenda and the frame controls a large part of what is thinkable

From gatekeeping to platforms

  • For much of the twentieth century a small number of editors decided what was newsworthy
  • That concentration had its own distortions
  • Platforms replaced it with engagement-maximizing algorithms
  • Concentration did not go away; it changed shape and added a profit motive

The penny press

  • The shift to mass media is older than the internet
  • Penny papers competed on crime, scandal, and nationalist outrage
  • Each escalation by one paper forced a matching response from the other
  • They did not set out to destabilize discourse; they wanted to sell newspapers

Agenda-setting

  • Media may not tell you what to think, but they tell you what to think about
  • Absence from the agenda is a form of power
  • If an issue is never covered, it is effectively not real for the audience
  • May Day became the workers’ holiday worldwide; the US and Canada moved Labor Day to September to avoid the association

Framing

  • How an issue is defined shapes which answers seem reasonable
  • “Screen time” versus “attention extraction”
  • “AI safety” versus “corporate accountability”
  • A reframe changes the feasible policy menu

The dependence effect [Galbraith1998]

  • Advertising manufactures the wants that production then satisfies
  • Bernays reframed women smoking as feminist defiance, and it worked
  • Wants are not a fixed input to the economy; they are produced
  • Recommendation systems are also preference-construction systems

Representation changes the thinkable

  • Nichelle Nichols’s Uhura showed a Black woman as a competent officer
  • MLK, Whoopi Goldberg, and Mae Jemison all cited that image
  • The Rooney Rule is a minimal intervention that changes who gets considered
  • What becomes thinkable depends partly on who is shown doing it

The propaganda model [Herman1988]

  • Five filters shape what gets through: ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, ideology
  • The range of acceptable debate is narrower than the range of actual opinion
  • This is manufacturing consent
  • The center of gravity of “respectable” debate is not where public opinion actually is

Five filters, updated

  • Engagement: what maximizes attention gets amplified
  • Algorithmic amplification: no editor, but a tuned ranking function
  • Influencer economies: distribution is bought and sold
  • The filters changed shape; the narrowing did not disappear

Narrative economics [Shiller2015]

  • Markets and beliefs move on contagious stories as much as on fundamentals
  • A bubble collapses when no greater fool can be found
  • Stories coordinate expectations faster than data
  • Whoever supplies the story steers the market

The ideology of the future

  • TESCREAL and longtermism let the already-powerful frame themselves as trustees of civilization [Torres2024]
  • They justify present costs for an unknowable future
  • Monism, one supreme value, is the trap
  • Framing AI’s builders as trustees forecloses other futures

Privacy as framing: Goffman [Goffman1959]

  • Front stage and back stage: a society without back stages is exhausting
  • Snowden’s disclosures had a measurable chilling effect on Google searches
  • Who gets to be private and who is watched is itself a contest
  • Privacy is not a single settled right; it is a set of claims being fought over

Surveillance is power

  • The Stasi had roughly 90,000 officers and 180,000 informants surveilling 16 million people
  • They had to coerce informants; we pay subscription fees to be surveilled
  • “Nothing to hide” is a privileged argument [Browne2015]
  • Surveillance rewires the people it surveils

Financial secrecy

  • Swiss banking law 1934 protected Nazi loot, including from Jewish depositors
  • Zucman estimates roughly $7.6 trillion sits offshore
  • The Panama and Pandora Papers show who actually gets privacy
  • The balance: privacy for persons, transparency for institutions

Epistemic fragmentation

  • Filter bubbles and polarization
  • The empirical echo-chamber story is more mixed than popular accounts claim
  • The larger problem may be a shared but distorted diet, not total separation
  • Disasters reveal mutual aid, not barbarism; media selection bias manufactures mistrust

Takeaway

  • Whoever controls the agenda and the frame controls a large part of what is thinkable
  • Platforms changed the economics of that production
  • And with it, the politics
