Change

Greg Wilson

2026

Opening question

  • Name one thing that is better now than it was fifty years ago
  • Who made it better, and how?
  • Change is slow, non-linear, and won by pressure, not persuasion alone

How change happens

  • Organization plus opportunity plus framing
  • Resource mobilization and political-process traditions agree on these three
  • The 3.5% threshold: in a city of half a million, that is seventeen thousand people doing something instead of complaining [Chenoweth2011]
  • Sustained, nonviolent mass participation is the lever

Kingdon’s policy window [Kingdon1995]

  • Three streams must align
    • Problem: is it defined as a crisis?
    • Politics: who is the constituency?
    • Policy: is there a ready solution?
  • Windows open rarely and close quickly

ACT UP

  • In 1987 ACT UP used direct action to accelerate drug approval
  • They changed FDA timelines through sustained pressure, not persuasion
  • The WGA’s 2023 strike won contract language that AI cannot write or rewrite scripts
  • Technologists’ leverage is real when it is organized

The expanding circle

  • Slave narratives by Equiano and Douglass were distributed as strategy
  • The 1790s sugar boycott was the first mass consumer boycott
  • Communication plus organized refusal changed what was acceptable
  • The audience was organized, not just informed

Kerala’s land reform

  • In 1969 Kerala won land reform by election, not occupation
  • Land went to the tenants who worked it
  • Literacy and life-expectancy gains followed once the landlord class’s veto was broken
  • A structural remedy can be won through ordinary politics

Worker power: won, not conceded

  • The Combination Acts, the Tolpuddle Martyrs, the 1926 General Strike
  • The eight-hour day came only after WWI’s fear of revolution
  • Workers achieved it by applying collective leverage at a moment of the opponents’ weakness
  • Gains are taken, then defended, then taken again

Institutions that made it stick

  • German codetermination gives workers half the board
  • Australia’s Harvester case (1907) set a “basic wage” through arbitration
  • COSATU helped end apartheid
  • Durable gains need institutions, not just moments of pressure

Rana Plaza

  • In 2013 the Rana Plaza collapse killed 1,134 garment workers
  • The Bangladesh Accord was a real gain, then weakened, then re-won
  • Two steps forward, one step back
  • Worker safety is won and defended, not granted

The technologist’s role

  • Insider/outsider power
  • Engineers can refuse, whistleblow, organize, build alternatives, or work on policy
  • Their leverage is unusual: scarcity and access
  • Their constraints are real: contracts, career risk, the ideology of neutrality

What technologists hold

  • Scarcity and access are cards few others hold
  • The yakuza declined not because they became less useful, but because association was made costly
  • A structurally powerful industry can be shrunk by sustained political decision
  • Refusing to engage simply delegates the decision to whoever will

The passion principle [Cech2021]

  • “Do what you love” becomes a way for employers to extract
  • Karoshi, death from overwork, is the Japanese endpoint
  • The software literature covers architecture and management but not workers’ rights or cooperatives
  • Medicine once hid its error rate of 44,000 to 98,000 preventable deaths a year; our field hides the same things

Applying the toolkit

  • Name the harms: attention extraction, misinformation, polarization, discrimination, labor deskilling, surveillance, concentration, environmental cost
  • Map each harm to the day’s lessons
  • Every real harm has structural, economic, psychological, regulatory, and ideological dimensions
  • If you cannot name the mechanism, you cannot change it

A realistic menu

  • Transparency and audits
  • Liability
  • Structural remedies: interoperability and breakups
  • Worker power
  • Public alternatives
  • Global coordination; be honest about capture risk for each

The five tools

  • Collective organizing
  • Regulatory pressure
  • Alternative structures
  • Strategic communication
  • Sustained participation
  • The Federalist Society ran a 40-year pipeline; the tools work for whoever uses them longest

Takeaway

  • The harms are not mysterious and the responses are not untried
  • Every tool discussed in the workshop is available to people who organize
  • Technologists hold real cards; the question is whether they play them
  • Amodei’s warning: AI severs the average person’s leverage through economic value
