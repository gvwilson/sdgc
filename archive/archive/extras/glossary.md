<span id="amateurism">amateurism</span>
:   The principle that athletes must not receive payment for athletic
    performance, historically used by organizations like the NCAA to
    restrict compensation.

<span id="audit_studies">audit studies</span>
:   A research methodology in which matched fictitious applications or
    inquiries are submitted to real decision-makers to measure
    differential treatment by race, gender, or other characteristics.

<span id="blind_selling">blind selling</span>
:   The historical Hollywood practice of requiring theater owners to
    commit to booking films before they had seen them.

<span id="block_booking">block booking</span>
:   The Hollywood practice of requiring exhibitors to license films in
    large bundles rather than individually, giving studios control
    over what theaters could show.

<span id="caveat_emptor">caveat emptor</span>
:   Latin for "let the buyer beware"; the legal doctrine that
    purchasers bear responsibility for assessing the quality and
    safety of goods before purchase.

<span id="conways_law">Conway's Law</span>
:   The observation, made by Melvin Conway in 1967, that organizations
    design systems that mirror their own communication structures.

<span id="culture_fit">culture fit</span>
:   The use of perceived compatibility with an existing team's norms
    and personality as a criterion in hiring decisions, which tends to
    reproduce the demographic composition of existing teams.

<span id="dynamic_obsolescence">dynamic obsolescence</span>
:   The rapid introduction of new product versions that make previous
    versions less functional or desirable, driving replacement
    purchases before the earlier product has stopped working.

<span id="functional_obsolescence">functional obsolescence</span>
:   The condition of a product that still operates but lacks features
    that have become standard, creating social and practical pressure
    to replace it.

<span id="implicit_bias">implicit bias</span>
:   Unconscious attitudes or stereotypes that influence decisions and
    behavior in ways the decision-maker does not recognize or intend.

<span id="institutional_knowledge">institutional knowledge</span>
:   Accumulated understanding of how an organization actually
    functions — its informal norms, histories, and relationships —
    that is not captured in formal documentation.

<span id="interchange_fee">interchange fee</span>
:   The fee paid by a merchant's bank to a cardholder's bank every
    time a card transaction is processed, ultimately passed on to
    merchants as a cost of accepting cards.

<span id="limited_liability">limited liability</span>
:   The legal principle that shareholders in a corporation cannot be
    held personally responsible for the company's debts or legal
    obligations beyond the amount they have invested.

<span id="luddites">Luddites</span>
:   Early nineteenth-century British textile workers who destroyed
    machinery to protest its use in circumventing labor agreements and
    replacing skilled workers; widely mischaracterized as opponents of
    technology as such.

<span id="nil_rights">Name, Image, and Likeness rights</span>
:   The right of college athletes to profit commercially from their
    own identity, established in the US in 2021 after decades of
    suppression by the NCAA's amateurism rules.

<span id="operant_conditioning">operant conditioning</span>
:   A learning process in which behavior is shaped through systems of
    rewards and punishments, associated with B.F. Skinner and applied
    in military training, game design, and platform engagement
    mechanics.

<span id="option_clause">option clause</span>
:   A contractual provision in Hollywood studio employment that
    allowed studios to renew an actor's or writer's contract
    unilaterally for additional terms, preventing them from working
    for other studios.

<span id="patent_medicine">patent medicine</span>
:   Proprietary, commercially marketed remedies of the nineteenth and
    early twentieth centuries, typically of unknown or misrepresented
    composition and often making fraudulent therapeutic claims.

<span id="planned_obsolescence">planned obsolescence</span>
:   The design of products with deliberately limited useful lifespans
    — through material choices, software locks, or withheld repairs —
    to encourage repeated purchases.

<span id="political_economy">political economy</span>
:   The study of the relationship between political institutions and
    economic systems, particularly how power shapes the distribution
    of resources and vice versa.

<span id="protection_racket">protection racket</span>
:   An extortion scheme in which payments are demanded in exchange for
    not inflicting the harm the extorting party itself threatens to
    impose.

<span id="psychological_obsolescence">psychological obsolescence</span>
:   The process by which products are made to feel outdated through
    styling changes and cultural messaging before their functional
    life has ended, driving replacement for social rather than
    practical reasons.

<span id="quasi_sovereign">quasi-sovereign</span>
:   Describing entities that exercise significant governmental or
    law-making power without being states, such as large technology
    platforms that set binding rules for billions of people across
    national jurisdictions.

<span id="right_to_repair">right to repair</span>
:   The principle that consumers and independent technicians should
    have the legal and practical ability to repair products they own
    without restriction by manufacturers through parts lockouts or
    software controls.

<span id="section_230">Section 230</span>
:   A provision of the US Communications Decency Act (1996) that
    protects internet platforms from civil liability for content
    posted by third-party users, foundational to the modern web.

<span id="sociotechnical">sociotechnical</span>
:   Relating to the interaction between social structures and
    technical systems, emphasizing that technology cannot be
    understood apart from its human and organizational context.

<span id="statistical_discrimination">statistical discrimination</span>
:   The practice of using group averages as proxies for individual
    characteristics in decisions about hiring, lending, or other
    allocations, producing disparate outcomes even without conscious
    prejudice.

<span id="taste_discrimination">taste discrimination</span>
:   Gary Becker's economic model in which employers who prefer not to
    hire members of certain groups effectively pay a premium for that
    preference, disadvantaging discriminating firms in competitive
    markets.

<span id="technology_transfer">technology transfer</span>
:   The process by which research or technology developed in publicly funded universities
    or government laboratories moves into commercial use by private firms, often without
    commensurate public benefit in the form of pricing constraints or revenue sharing.

<span id="transboundary_pollution">transboundary pollution</span>
:   Environmental contamination that originates in one jurisdiction
    but causes harm in another, creating regulatory and liability gaps
    that allow polluters to externalize costs across borders.

<span id="trips">TRIPS agreement</span>
:   The Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights
    agreement, a WTO framework that extended patent and copyright
    protections globally, significantly affecting access to medicines
    in developing countries.

<span id="vision_zero">Vision Zero</span>
:   A traffic safety policy framework originating in Sweden that
    treats all traffic fatalities as preventable and designs
    infrastructure and regulation around that premise rather than
    accepting casualties as inevitable.
