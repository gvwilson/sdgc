# Unused lesson material

## 1) Deciding what is harmful and how to regulate it

- Privacy is contested like sex: the unit of privacy was the *household*, not
  the person; "the personal is political"; marital rape was legal in most US
  states until the 1970s-80s. (`privacy-power-self`)
- Sodomy laws are a Victorian legal export (1861/1885, Oscar Wilde) spread
  through colonial law, not an ancient universal. (`finale`)
- The five-step uncertainty playbook: fund scientists, insist correlation is
  not causation, call regulation an attack on freedom, create neutral-sounding
  front groups, delay. (`regulation-that-worked`)
- The four-phase industry response: denial to uncertainty to market argument to
  acceptance (compliance always turns out cheaper than predicted).
  (`regulation-that-worked`)
- Kefauver-Harris (1962): before it, you could sell a drug in the US without
  proving it did anything. (`regulation-that-worked`)
- The 1998 Master Settlement Agreement: states sued to recover $206B in Medicaid
  costs, but this was a settlement, not a solution. (`regulation-that-worked`)
- Minamata: Chisso's mercury, with cats as the ignored early warning; the
  Sandoz/Rhine spill (1986) produced the binding Rhine Action Programme.
  (`regulation-that-worked`)
- Wirecard: Germany's regulator attacked the *journalist* instead of the fraud;
  Korean chaebol executives were convicted then pardoned as too important to
  jail. (`a-little-psychology`, `harmful-models`)
- The moral-panic lineage (dime novels to Wertham's comics to rock to D&D) and the
  cross-national check (Japan/South Korea: high game use, low violence). The
  real harm was loot boxes, not gunfire. (`regulation-that-worked`)
- Parfit's non-identity problem: harms to "future people" can't name individual
  victims; this is the philosophical twin of diffuse harm. (`what-we-owe-the-future`)

## 2) Power and institutions

- The 1886 US railroad gauge change: an "incompatibility" was a market weapon:
  southern carriers profited from unloading/reloading freight at the gauge
  boundary, so the "technical" choice was political. (`how-we-got-here`)
- The "Delaware flip": Canadian startups re-incorporate in Delaware not because
  Delaware law is better but because US VCs require it: pure path dependence
  with no operational benefit. (`how-we-got-here`)
- Platforms act as quasi-governments: eBay adjudicates disputes, App Store
  review is the only appeal, Facebook makes de facto regulatory decisions in
  other countries; the yakuza supplied 2011 Tohoku disaster relief faster than
  the state. (`harmful-models`)
- "We don't do politics here" usually means the winners already have what they
  want and can describe their arrangement as natural. (`inequality`)
- Selectorate theory: leaders distribute to a small winning coalition, not the
  whole "family"; Pfeffer found political skill predicts advancement better than
  technical competence. (`inequality`)
- Tithing analogy: the App Store's 30% cut is a tithe; deplatforming is
  excommunication (removal from the infrastructure needed to do business).
  (`harmful-models`)
- Philanthrocapitalism: donor-advised funds (~$230B) and the Giving Pledge
  convert taxable wealth into permanently controlled endowments; the Gates
  Foundation was at one point the WHO's second-largest funder. (`inequality`)
- Legibility (James C. Scott): surnames, house numbers, and passports exist so
  the state can tax, draft, and police; South Africa's pass laws became the
  world's first biometric register (1986). (`privacy-power-self`)
- Wells Fargo fired 5,300 staff over fake accounts ("eight is great"): mass
  wrongdoing flows from impossible quotas, not from bad individuals.
  (`a-little-psychology`)
- Self-fulfilling design: terms of service written for adversaries and
  moderation that treats everyone as a suspect produce the distrust they assume
  (the contrast with Ostrom's governed commons). (`a-little-psychology`)

## 3) Markets are made, not natural

- Graeber's barter myth: credit and debt predate money; money emerged to settle
  debts, not to simplify barter. (`how-we-got-here`)
- Limited liability is a grant from the state (Dutch East India Company, 1602),
  not a natural feature of commerce. (`how-we-got-here`)
- Enshittification (Doctorow) with a concrete ladder: Booking.com's commission
  12% to 25-30%, Columbia House's penny records, JanSport's backpacks,
  YouTube's 2018 monetization threshold. (`how-we-got-here`, `more-analogies`)
- Public subsidy, private profit: every technology in the iPhone was
  government-funded (DARPA internet, GPS, Siri/SRI); the mRNA vaccine produced
  Moderna's ~$200B while the NIH's IP claim went unenforced; Apple paid 0.005%
  tax on Irish profits. (`how-we-got-here`)
- The Statute of Anne (1710) created copyright to break the Stationers'
  monopoly, not to protect authors; the 1998 term extension was timed to keep
  Mickey Mouse out of the public domain. (`how-we-got-here`)
- "Homo economicus" is a political argument dressed as math: Hobbes to Spencer's
  "survival of the fittest" to the rational agent. (`a-little-psychology`)
- Ostrom vs Hardin: Hardin described an *unmanaged* commons he never studied;
  Ostrom's eight design principles (Nobel 2009) show communities govern shared
  resources without markets or the state. (`a-little-psychology`)
- Apple's green-bubble lock-in: internal documents admit RCS would reduce the
  social cost of switching; the DMA forced support in 2024. (`how-we-got-here`)
- Institutional variety beyond the shareholder firm: the Islamic waqf (approx. a
  third of Ottoman farmland), keiretsu, the Mittelstand, the Hindu Undivided
  Family (Tata/Birla). (`control-and-alternatives`)
- MLM math: the FTC found <1% of participants earned a net profit; Amazon
  Basics competes with its own sellers using their sales data; Uber pitched
  drivers as "entrepreneurs." (`harmful-models`)

## 4) How people actually think

- Loss aversion and hyperbolic discounting: taxi drivers work longer on bad
  days; "our future selves are strangers" (gym memberships, quitting smoking).
  (`a-little-psychology`)
- Nudge proof: pension auto-enrollment 50-60% to 80-90% (UK 2012, ~10M workers);
  the Behavioural Insights Team's "nine out of ten pay on time" tax letter.
  (`a-little-psychology`)
- Status beats income: Norway's public tax records widened the well-being gap
  29% (Perez-Truglia); Robert Frank's local-rank finding. (`a-little-psychology`)
- "People want data but believe stories": public-health campaigns use named
  people, not statistics; industries fund data-heavy think tanks for the
  *appearance* of rigor. (`a-little-psychology`, `finale`)
- Moral disengagement (Bandura): "user data as exhaust" and "engagement
  optimization" are euphemistic labels that let good people do harmful work.
  (`a-little-psychology`, `more-psychology`)

## 5) Inequality and stratification

- "Meritocracy" was coined by Michael Young in a 1958 *satire*; he spent the
  rest of his life pointing out the misuse. (`more-psychology`)
- Merit-pay organizations show *larger* gender pay gaps (Castilla & Benard's
  moral licensing); believing in meritocracy predicts harsher judgment of the
  poor. (`more-psychology`)
- Audit studies: identical resumes with white-coded names get consistently more
  callbacks; Rivera's "polished" vs "rough around the edges" is class, not
  competence. (`more-psychology`)
- Amazon's internal recruiting tool (abandoned 2018) penalized resumes
  containing "women's." (`more-psychology`)
- Women's share of computing was *higher* in the 1980s than today: the
  "pipeline" explanation is contradicted by the evidence. (`more-psychology`)
- Wilkinson & Pickett: more unequal societies do worse on nearly every social
  indicator; the mechanism is status anxiety. (`a-little-psychology`)
- Poverty depletes cognitive bandwidth like sleep deprivation (Mullainathan &
  Shafir); McIntosh's invisible knapsack (why "just try harder" fails).
  (`inequality`)
- Credit bureaus collect without consent and profit whether the data is
  accurate or not (Equifax 2017: ~147M exposed, sold its own monitoring, ~$few-
  dollar settlements); accuracy is not fairness. (`inequality`)
- Federici: the witch trials disciplined female labor as enclosure removed its
  economic basis. (`control-and-alternatives`)
- Becker's 1957 prediction that competition eliminates discrimination was
  falsified; favored-group workers benefit from exclusion (LBJ: "give him
  somebody to look down on"). (`more-psychology`)

## 6) Media, ideology, and public opinion

- Galbraith's "dependence effect": advertising manufactures the wants that
  production then satisfies. (`more-psychology`)
- Representation changes what's thinkable: Nichelle Nichols/Uhura (MLK, Whoopi
  Goldberg, Mae Jemison); the Rooney Rule as a minimal intervention.
  (`more-psychology`)
- Goffman's front/back stage: "a society without back stages is one where
  performance is continuous and exhausting"; Snowden's measurable chilling
  effect on Google searches. (`privacy-power-self`)
- "Paying for the privilege": the Stasi had to coerce informants; we pay
  subscription fees to be surveilled. (`privacy-power-self`)
- The first privacy right (Warren & Brandeis, 1890) protected the prominent
  from gossip, not the powerless from the state; "nothing to hide" is a
  privileged argument (Simone Browne). (`privacy-power-self`)
- Financial secrecy protects power: Swiss banking law 1934 (Jewish depositors to
  Nazi loot), Zucman's ~$7.6T offshore, the Panama/Pandora Papers.
  (`privacy-power-self`)
- "Privacy for persons, transparency for institutions" (India's RTI Act 2005,
  South Africa's TRC, and the double edge of the right-to-be-forgotten).
  (`privacy-power-self`)
- Disasters reveal mutual aid, not barbarism (Quarantelli/Solnit): "elite panic"
  vs ordinary generosity; media selection bias manufactures mistrust.
  (`change-happens`)
- TESCREAL/longtermism (Torres): Musk's and OpenAI's "trustees of civilization"
  framing lets the already-powerful justify present costs for an unknowable
  future. (`what-we-owe-the-future`)
- Narrative economics (Shiller): markets move on contagious stories; Edison
  electrocuted animals to frame alternating current as lethal.
  (`more-psychology`, `how-we-got-here`)

## 7) How change happens

- Rosa Parks was NAACP secretary and a Highlander graduate; the boycott was run
  by the Montgomery Improvement Association and she was *chosen* as plaintiff;
  the "great person" story is wrong and strategically disabling.
  (`change-happens`, `finale`)
- ACT UP (1987) used direct action to accelerate drug approval: activists
  changed FDA timelines. (`finale`)
- The expanding circle: novels trained empathy (Lynn Hunt), slave narratives
  (Equiano, Douglass) were distributed as strategy, and the 1790s sugar boycott
  was the first mass consumer boycott. (`finale`)
- Union wins were not conceded: the Combination Acts, Tolpuddle Martyrs, the
  1926 General Strike, Thatcher, and the eight-hour day came only after WWI's
  fear of revolution. (`control-and-alternatives`)
- Worker-power institutions: German codetermination (workers hold half the
  board), Australia's arbitration/"basic wage" (Harvester 1907), COSATU's role
  in ending apartheid. (`control-and-alternatives`)
- Rana Plaza (1,134 dead, 2013) to the Bangladesh Accord: a real gain, then
  weakened, then re-won (two steps forward, one step back).
  (`control-and-alternatives`)
- The passion principle (Cech): "do what you love" lets employers extract;
  *karoshi* ("death from overwork") is its Japanese endpoint.
  (`control-and-alternatives`)
- Tech's taboo: software-engineering literature covers architecture and
  management but not workers' rights or cooperatives: as medicine once hid its
  error rate (44k-98k preventable deaths/year). (`control-and-alternatives`)
- Kerala's 1969 land reform (by election, not occupation) to literacy and
  life-expectancy gains once the landlord class's veto was broken.
  (`change-happens`)
- Dario Amodei's own warning: AI severs "the average person's leverage through
  economic value"; the material basis of democracy. (`what-we-owe-the-future`)
- The finale's five tools as an alternative reform-menu taxonomy: collective
  organizing, regulatory pressure, alternative structures, strategic
  communication, sustained participation (the Federalist Society's 40-year
  pipeline). (`finale`)
- "Refusing to vote or join a union is a political act"; refusing to engage
  simply delegates the decision to whoever will. (`change-happens`)
- DMA 2024 (gatekeepers, fines up to 20% of global revenue) and India's
  UPI / Brazil's Pix as "public rails before dominance." (`regulation-that-worked`)

## Closing synthesis (25 min)

- Welsh 1987 tree-planting: 700,000 trees that no one who planted will live to
  see mature. (`what-we-owe-the-future`)
- Berlin's "crooked timber of humanity": monism (one supreme value) is the root
  of tyranny; a warning the longtermist frame triggers. (`what-we-owe-the-future`, `inequality`)
- "Don't foreclose options" is the most defensible version of what we owe the
  future. (`what-we-owe-the-future`)
- The introduction's blackboard anecdote: the visitor who wrote "I don't think a
  future built by you will be better than what we have today" after being cut
  off three times. (`intro`)
